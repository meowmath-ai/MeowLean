# JSP-000377 · Planar distances away from integers

For every $\delta>0$, there is $C(\delta)>0$ such that any finite point set in a planar disk of radius $X\ge1$ has cardinality less than $C(\delta)\sqrt X$ if each distance between distinct points differs from every integer by at least $\delta$.

For fixed $\delta$, dividing the bound by $X$ gives a quantity tending to zero. Also, for every $\varepsilon>0$, $C(\delta)\sqrt X\le X^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ for all sufficiently large $X$. Thus the bound answers both asymptotic questions in Erdős 465.

The original upper-bound questions appear in Erdős, *Some of My Favourite Problems Which Recently Have Been Solved*, §9, pp. 67–68. The complete mathematical proof is S. V. Konyagin, *About Distances between Points on the Plane*, Matematicheskie Zametki **69** (2001), 630–633, Theorem p. 630, with English translation in Mathematical Notes **69** (2001), 578–581. [Original problems](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1982-33.pdf#page=9) · [Konyagin's paper and full text](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/mzm691) · [English edition](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1010276601734)

The Lean argument constructs a radial kernel from positive combinations of angular integrals. Its sum over ordered pairs is nonnegative, while pairs far enough apart and away from integer distances make uniformly negative contributions of order $r^{-1/2}$. The pairwise distance condition implies separation, so a uniform packing estimate bounds each point's number of nearby neighbors. Comparing the positive and negative contributions gives the square-root bound. A Euclidean isometry transfers the complex-plane calculation to the standard real plane.
