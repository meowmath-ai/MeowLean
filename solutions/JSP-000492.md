# JSP-000492 · Repeated distances on a sphere

For every sufficiently large $n$, there are $n$ points on the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^3$ with $\Omega(n^{4/3})$ unordered pairs at chord length $\sqrt 2$. The distance is independent of $n$.

For a positive integer $k$, take the planar grid of points $(x,y)$ with $1\le x\le k$ and $1\le y\le 2k^2$, and take the lines $y=ax+b$ with $1\le a\le k$ and $1\le b\le k^2$, all parameters integral. Each line meets the grid at each of the $k$ allowed abscissas, since $1\le ax+b\le2k^2$. There are $2k^3$ grid points, $k^3$ lines, and $k^4$ incidences.

Map each point to the unit normalization of $(x,y,1)$ and each line to the unit normalization of $(a,-1,b)$. Both maps are injective. A normalized vector determines the underlying positive ray, and the coordinate fixed at $1$ or $-1$ determines the representative on that ray. The two images are disjoint by the sign of their second coordinate. At an incidence, the two vectors have zero dot product, so the corresponding unit vectors have squared distance $2$. The disjoint images and the two injections ensure that the $k^4$ incidences yield $k^4$ distinct unordered pairs.

For $n\ge3$, use the largest $k$ with $3k^3\le n$. Add arbitrary distinct unused points on the sphere until the set has $n$ points. All counted pairs persist. Since $n<3(k+1)^3\le24k^3$, the configuration has at least $(n/24)^{4/3}$ such pairs. The finitely many smaller sizes can take a zero lower bound in the superlinear formulation.

Erdős asks whether the maximum number of equal-distance pairs on a two-dimensional sphere, divided by the number of points, tends to infinity. This is the formulation in §I, p. 2 of his 1985 paper and in the prize catalog. Erdős, Hickerson and Pach give the published lower bound in Theorem 2(ii), p. 572.
