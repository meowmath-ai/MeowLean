# JSP-001010 · Optimal residue-class coverage

For $N\ge1$, let $F(N)$ be the maximum, over choices of one class modulo each $d=1,\ldots,N$, of the minimum coverage count on $1,\ldots,N$. Then $F(N)\sim\log N$. For real $x$, the same asymptotic holds with $N=\lfloor x\rfloor$.

## Upper bound

For any choice of classes, the class modulo $d$ contains at most $N/d+1$ of the $N$ integers. Summing coverages over the interval and dividing by $N$ bounds the minimum by $H_N+1$, where $H_N=\sum_{d=1}^N 1/d$. Thus $F(N)\le\log N+2$.

## Lower bound

Fix $0<t<1/2$, set $q=e^{-t}$, and use the first $K=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ moduli. If $c_m$ is the current coverage at $m$, consider the potential $\sum_{m=1}^N q^{c_m}$, initially $N$. For the next modulus $d$, average the updated potential over its $d$ possible classes. Each $m$ is hit for exactly one choice, so this average is the current potential multiplied by $1-(1-q)/d$. Choose a class with updated potential at most the average. Repeating gives

$$
\sum_{m=1}^N q^{c_m}
\le N\prod_{d=1}^{K}\left(1-\frac{1-q}{d}\right)
\le N\exp\bigl(-(1-q)H_K\bigr).
$$

Let $k=\lfloor(1-2t)\log N\rfloor$ and let $B$ consist of the integers with $c_m<k$. Each of these integers contributes at least $q^k$ to the potential. Consequently

$$
|B|\le N\exp\bigl(tk-(1-e^{-t})H_K\bigr).
$$

For $N\ge2$, $H_K\ge\log(K+1)\ge\log N-\log2$. Using $1-e^{-t}\ge t-t^2/2$ and $tk\le(t-2t^2)\log N$, the last bound is at most $2N^{1-3t^2/2}$. For fixed $t>0$, it follows that $k|B|=o(N)$. For sufficiently large $N$, reserve $k$ different moduli in $\{K+1,\dots,N\}$ for each $m\in B$, with no modulus reserved twice. At each reserved modulus choose the class of its assigned integer. Choose any class at the remaining moduli. Every integer in $B$ receives at least $k$ hits, and every other integer already had at least $k$.

It follows that $\liminf F(N)/\log N\ge1-2t$. Letting $t$ decrease to zero and using the upper bound proves the limit. For real $x$, the sets of allowed moduli and covered integers are both determined by $\lfloor x\rfloor$, and $\log\lfloor x\rfloor/\log x\to1$.

The problem appears in Erdős, *A Survey of Problems in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980), §6, item 6, p. 108. This proof uses a deterministic averaging argument for the initial choice, followed by the reserved-modulus construction.
