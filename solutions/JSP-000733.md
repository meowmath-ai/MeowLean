# JSP-000733 · Primitive subset sums

Let $R(N)$ be the largest cardinality of a set $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ such that divisibility between two nonempty subset sums of $A$ implies equality of their numerical values. Then $R(N)\sim\log_2 N$ as $N\to\infty$.

## Counting upper bound

Suppose two different subsets have the same sum. Removing their intersection leaves two disjoint subsets with that same reduced sum. Positivity of the elements makes both subsets nonempty, and their combined sum is twice the reduced sum, contradicting the divisibility condition. Thus all $2^k$ subset sums of an admissible set of size $k$, including the empty sum, differ. They belong to $\{0,\ldots,kN\}$, so $2^k\le kN+1$.

For $k=R(N)>0$ and $N\ge1$, logarithms give $k\le\log_2 N+\log_2 k+1$. Since $\log_2 k\le k/2$ for sufficiently large $k$, this first gives $k=O(\log N)$ and then $k\le \log_2 N+O(\log\log N)$.

## Binary lower bound

For $m=\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor$ and $M=2^m$, use the $m$ integers $M-2^i$ with $0\le i<m$. For a subset of indices, write $x$ for the sum of its powers of two and $s(x)$ for the number of ones in its binary expansion. The corresponding subset sum is $g(x)=Ms(x)-x$.

Suppose $g(y)=c\,g(x)$ for $0<x,y<M$ and an integer $c\ge2$. The equality gives $cx=y+qM$ and $s(y)=cs(x)-q$ for some integer $q\ge0$. Write

$$
H(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left\lfloor z/2^j\right\rfloor.
$$

For $0\le z<M$, the binary expansion gives $H(z)=z-s(z)$, while $H(y+qM)=H(y)+q(M-1)$. Consequently $H(cx)=cH(x)$. Every summand of $H(cx)$ is at least $c$ times the corresponding summand of $H(x)$. At $j=1+v_2(x)$ the fractional part of $x/2^j$ is $1/2$, so that summand is strictly larger for $c\ge2$. This is a contradiction. The set is admissible, hence $R(N)\ge m>\log_2 N-1$. Combining the bounds gives the limit.

The binary construction appears in Erdős, Lev, Rauzy, Sándor and Sárközy, *Greedy Algorithm, Arithmetic Progressions, Subset Sums and Divisibility*, Discrete Mathematics **200** (1999), Theorem 5, p. 129 and proof in §8, pp. 133–134. The formalization uses the construction and the elementary counting bound to obtain the leading asymptotic.
